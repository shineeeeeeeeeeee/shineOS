# ART BIBLE — is-not.cool

**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Source of truth for all visual assets  
**Last updated:** Phase 1

---

## 1. Overall Art Style

The visual identity is **cozy retro computing** rendered as **hand-illustrated 2D artwork**.

Every asset should feel like it was drawn by a thoughtful illustrator and then carefully colored and textured. The world is whimsical, warm, and slightly quirky — like stepping into a friendly indie game or a well-loved children's book about computers.

The style is **not** photorealistic, not 3D, not flat vector, and not generic SaaS illustration. It is **illustrated**, **textured**, and **tactile**.

---

## 2. Color Philosophy

- **Warm, muted, and inviting.** The palette is rooted in cream, beige, soft terracotta, dusty teal, and warm gray.
- **Colorful but restrained.** Accent colors are used sparingly and intentionally. A single bright color can carry a scene.
- **No neon. No harsh gradients. No clinical whites.**
- **Sky:** Soft teal-blue, like a gentle afternoon. Not saturated, not gray.
- **Clouds:** White to very pale gray, with soft shadows. They are large, pillowy, and friendly.
- **Computer/objects:** Cream, beige, warm grays, with small pops of color (stickers, indicator lights, keys).
- **UI within the OS:** Uses the same warm palette. Window backgrounds are off-white, not pure white. Borders are warm gray, not black.

---

## 3. Lighting

- **Soft, diffused, overhead.** Like a window on a cloudy day.
- **No harsh shadows. No dramatic rim light. No cinematic noir.**
- **Shadows are warm gray, slightly transparent, and soft-edged.**
- **Highlights are gentle.** A subtle shine on the computer case, a soft glow on a screen.
- **Atmospheric.** A faint haze or warmth near the horizon. The world feels airy and open.

---

## 4. Line Quality

- **Hand-drawn, slightly imperfect lines.**
- **Line weight varies.** Outlines are not uniform — some lines are thicker for emphasis, thinner for detail.
- **Lines are dark warm gray or deep brown, never pure black.**
- **Sketchy texture is welcome** in background elements and organic shapes (clouds, leaves).
- **Cleaner, more precise lines** for UI elements and the computer itself, but still with a human touch.

---

## 5. Texture

- **Subtle paper or canvas texture** overlays the entire world. It should be barely noticeable but adds warmth and tactility.
- **Noise and grain** are acceptable in moderation. The goal is analog warmth, not digital sterility.
- **Objects have material texture:** the computer has a slight plastic or metal grain, the desk has wood grain, leaves have vein texture.
- **Avoid smooth, flat, vector-perfect surfaces** unless the object is explicitly meant to be digital (screens, UI).

---

## 6. Shape Language

- **Organic and rounded.** Clouds are pillowy. The computer is chunky and friendly. Plants have soft, leafy curves.
- **Slightly imperfect circles and blobs.** Nothing is mathematically perfect.
- **Chunky proportions** for the computer and major props. They feel solid and huggable.
- **UI shapes** are rounded but not excessively so. Corners are soft, not pill-shaped.
- **Asymmetry is welcome** in decorative elements (stickers, plants, desk clutter).

---

## 7. Computer Design

- **Vintage cream/beige CRT or all-in-one computer.** Chunky, substantial, with a thick bezel.
- **Screen:** Glowing softly. The content inside is the OS, which has its own visual identity (see Section 11).
- **Stickers:** A few tasteful, quirky stickers on the case. Hand-drawn style. Small, colorful, personal.
- **Ports and details:** Subtle, not overwhelming. A power button, maybe a disk drive slot, a few ports.
- **Keyboard and mouse:** Matching cream/beige. Chunky keys. The mouse is rounded and friendly.
- **Cable:** A single, slightly messy cable trailing off the desk. Adds life and imperfection.
- **Screen glow:** A soft colored halo around the monitor when it's on.

---

## 8. Cloud / Environment Design

- **Large, pillowy, illustrated clouds.** They are the primary environmental element.
- **Clouds have soft shadows and subtle highlights.** They feel volumetric but stylized.
- **Teal-blue sky background.** Gradient from lighter near the horizon to slightly deeper at the top. Very subtle.
- **The computer floats among the clouds** or sits on a small floating desk/platform among them.
- **Depth is created** by layering clouds of different sizes, opacities, and warm/cool tones.
- **No harsh edges.** Everything blends softly into the sky.
- **Optional:** A faint sun or warm light source off-screen, creating a gentle glow.

---

## 9. Props

Props make the world feel lived-in and tell a story.

- **Plants:** Small potted plants on the desk. Simple, illustrated leaves in soft greens. Terracotta or cream pots.
- **Mug:** A chunky ceramic mug, possibly with a simple pattern or sticker. Steam rising subtly.
- **Books/notebooks:** Stacked or scattered. Cream covers, simple titles or patterns.
- **Lamp:** A small, friendly desk lamp with a warm glow.
- **Stickers:** On the computer, on the mug, on the books. Quirky, hand-drawn, small.
- **Cable:** As mentioned, a single trailing cable.
- **Small toys or figurines:** Optional. Adds personality.
- **All props share the same illustration style:** hand-drawn, textured, warm, slightly imperfect.

---

## 10. Character Design

If characters appear (e.g., a cursor companion, a desktop pet, game characters):

- **Simple, rounded, and expressive.** Think small animal or blob-like creature.
- **Large eyes or a single expressive feature.** Minimal detail, maximum personality.
- **Warm colors** that complement the palette.
- **No complex anatomy. No photorealism.**
- **Movement is bouncy and playful.** Squash and stretch is welcome.
- **Characters should feel like they belong in the same hand-illustrated world.**

---

## 11. UI Relationship to the Artwork

The OS UI must feel like it **emerges from** the illustrated world, not like a generic interface pasted on top.

- **Window chrome** uses the same warm grays and soft shadows as the environment.
- **Icons** are hand-drawn or hand-drawn-inspired. Not generic system icons.
- **Typography** inside the OS uses the project's UI font (Inter / system) for readability, but display text can use the handwritten font.
- **Selection and focus states** use the warm accent color, not a harsh blue.
- **The desktop wallpaper** inside the OS could be a simplified, zoomed-in version of the cloud world.
- **Menus and docks** have soft, organic shapes. Rounded corners, subtle shadows, warm borders.

---

## 12. Game Artwork Direction

Mini-games inside the OS must feel like **hand-crafted paper games** or **pocket adventure games**.

- **Pixel art is acceptable** if it matches the warm, limited palette.
- **Vector/flat illustration** is acceptable if it uses the same line quality and color philosophy.
- **All game assets** must feel like they were made by the same illustrator.
- **UI within games** uses the same window chrome and button styles as the OS.
- **Game worlds** can be simpler than the main world but must not break the visual contract.

---

## 13. What Future Illustrations MUST Preserve

- **Warm, cream/beige/teal color foundation.**
- **Hand-illustrated, slightly imperfect line quality.**
- **Soft, diffused lighting with warm shadows.**
- **Subtle texture overlay (paper/canvas feel).**
- **Chunky, friendly proportions for major objects.**
- **Organic, pillowy shapes for clouds and natural elements.**
- **Quirky, personal details (stickers, small props, environmental storytelling).**
- **Cozy, nostalgic, whimsical emotional tone.**
- **Consistent visual hierarchy: environment → computer → OS → apps → games.**

---

## 14. What Future Illustrations MUST Avoid

- **Pure black lines or pure white backgrounds.**
- **Harsh gradients, neon colors, or clinical palettes.**
- **Photorealistic textures or 3D rendering.**
- **Generic SaaS or landing-page illustration style.**
- **Flat, lifeless vector art without texture or line variation.**
- **Overly detailed or cluttered compositions.**
- **Sharp, aggressive, or cold visual tones.**
- **Copying existing game or software visual identities.**
- **Inconsistent line weights or color temperatures across assets.**
- **Excessive rounded corners on everything (UI or art).**

---
